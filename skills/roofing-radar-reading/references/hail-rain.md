# Hail, rain, precipitation totals and flooding

Research update: 2026-10-11. This is a decision procedure with partially verified source support, not a validated hazard algorithm. The source register below distinguishes successfully read training material from unavailable product specifications. Do not report a current reading without current evidence for the requested place and time.

## Ask what must be established

| Question | Evidence and method | Why the distinction matters |
|---|---|---|
| **What** is the claimed quantity? | Identify hail occurrence/diameter, rain rate, interval precipitation amount, precipitation probability, flood stage/inundation or roof moisture. Preserve measured, reported, estimated and forecast labels. | Different products answer different questions; a vivid radar pixel is not a measurement of all these quantities. |
| **Where** does it apply? | Record radar sample height/range, grid footprint, report precision, gauge site/exposure, basin and job geometry. Check actual product coverage separately for CONUS, Alaska, Hawaii and territories. | A grid, point observation, drainage basin and roof have different spatial support. A product outside its domain is unavailable, not zero. |
| **When** does it apply? | Preserve scan/observation time, accumulation start/end, issue time, receipt time, source revision and decision cutoff. Check field-specific times before spatial comparisons. | A newly retrieved swath can describe old hail; a storm-total or rolling-hour amount cannot describe the instantaneous rate. |
| **How** was it obtained? | Identify radar/dual-pol algorithm, MESH version and flavor, radar-only versus gauge-adjusted QPE, gauge instrument or forecast model. Audit units, flags, quality, upstream inputs and missing samples. | Values from different methods are not automatically comparable or independent. |
| **Why** is the interpretation justified? | State the supporting evidence, strongest plausible alternative and the observation that would distinguish them. Apply the evidence ladder below. | An explanation must survive alternatives such as melting snow, hail-contaminated rainfall estimates, radar overshoot or report-location error. |

## Keep the quantities separate

| Quantity | Allowed interpretation | Forbidden shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Radar reflectivity, dBZ | A radar scattering-related quantity whose interpretation depends on particle populations and sampling; use other fields and environment. | Exact rain rate or hail diameter from color, or hail whenever reflectivity is high. |
| Rain rate, such as mm/h | Estimated or measured rate for the product's stated sampling period and footprint. Record whether effectively instantaneous or interval averaged. | Assuming that the rate continued for an hour, or treating mm/h as mm. |
| QPE: quantitative precipitation estimate | An estimate of precipitation already occurring/occurred over the named interval. Identify liquid equivalent, method and revisions. | Calling it a future forecast, a direct gauge measurement or confirmed water at the roof. |
| Gauge precipitation amount | Instrument observation over its actual interval at its site, subject to instrument/exposure/QC limitations. | Assuming it represents every neighboring grid cell or treating every gauge as perfect truth. |
| QPF: quantitative precipitation forecast | Forecast amount over its valid interval, with issue/run, lead time and uncertainty. | Calling it observed rainfall or turning its amount into a probability. |
| PoP: probability of precipitation | Under the cited NWS explanation, chance that the forecast point/grid receives at least 0.01 inch of rain during the specified forecast period; preserve each provider's documented threshold and precipitation definition. [S1] | Percent of work hours, fraction of a roof that gets wet, storm severity, or a rainfall amount. |
| Flooding, flood stage or inundation | A separate hydrologic outcome. Use official warnings, basin conditions, stream observations/forecasts and validated inundation products as applicable. [S4] | Rainfall amount directly converted to water depth, road passability or parcel inundation. |
| Roof wetness or substrate moisture | A site/material observation or a separately validated moisture method with stated limitations. | Forecast rain, radar precipitation or nearby humidity presented as a measured dry/wet roof or authorization to install. |

The cited PoP example uses rain. For snow or mixed precipitation, verify the selected product’s measurable-precipitation/liquid-equivalent definition; 0.01 inch in that example is not a snow-depth threshold.

Terms such as "heavy rain" require a named operational definition or qualitative wording. Do not promote a threshold in an old training illustration into a universal rain-intensity standard. Precipitation phase also matters: liquid-equivalent QPE is not snow depth or ice-accretion thickness.

For a map click, preserve the requested latitude/longitude and time, then show the source footprint or station distance and the selected time mode: **current**, **forecast** or **past**. A precise coordinate does not make a grid estimate a precise observation at that coordinate. Name the sampled grid cell or documented interpolation method; do not introduce false detail by enlarging pixels. A nearest gauge is an observation at that station, with its distance and interval, not a measurement at the clicked roof. Current requires valid sufficiently recent observations, forecast requires an issuance available at the chosen cutoff and a valid period appropriate to the requested time, and past requires archived observation/estimate intervals and revision status. Show separate cards when evidence types coexist; an observation at one time cannot establish conditions at a different time. A point falling outside the product's supported mask is unavailable even when the map can display it.

## Hail procedure

1. **Check official warnings independently.** Preserve the official hazard, area and valid period. A warned hail size is a warning statement, not a measured diameter at each point. Response to an active warning must not wait for a hail report or a dual-pol confirmation.
2. **Establish adequate sampling.** Apply radar-procedure.md to height, range, terrain, source switching, missing gates and artifacts. Identify relevant freezing/melting-level information with its source/time if using it. Radar samples hail/mixtures aloft; melting, drift and sampling limit the link to surface hail.
3. **Challenge the high-reflectivity explanation.** Compare co-located, time-matched reflectivity, CC/rhoHV, ZDR and KDP, elevation slices, evolution and environmental context. NWS examples show that high reflectivity can occur with either heavy rain or hail/rain mixtures. [S2, S3] Do not apply those examples' numerical ranges as universal classifiers. Melting hail or graupel can produce rain-like polarimetric signatures; wet snow can reduce CC. [S5] Low CC alone is neither a hail confirmation nor proof of tornado debris. A hydrometeor classification describes an algorithm's sampled volume, not a roof-level observation.
4. **Read the exact hail-estimate product.** Capture provider, upstream lineage, product identifier, algorithm/version/flavor, units, grid, masking and valid time/window. MESH is an estimate of maximum hail size derived from radar-based information; it is not an observed diameter. Product metadata is required before a number can be interpreted. If a flavor uses percentile-style naming, consult that calibration definition; a label is not automatically a calibrated probability of hail at a roof or a confidence interval on its diameter. Current MESH version/flavor and cadence documentation were unavailable in this research session. [U1, U2]
5. **Separate current fields from swath maxima.** A maximum over a named window means the maximum estimate during that window at the grid cell, not hail at the window end and not the amount/duration of hail. Successive rolling maxima overlap: do not sum them or count each as a new event. A long-window swath may combine different storms; establish event identity before attribution. Compare like versions and windows, or document a validated conversion; do not manufacture one.
6. **Seek surface corroboration with provenance.** Prefer a documented diameter measurement with scale, event time, precise location and observer/instrument context. An object-comparison report is an estimate; retain its original wording and precision. Check preliminary/revised status, duplicated provider copies, rounded times and town-level geocoding. A report near a cell is supporting evidence at that report's place/time, not a sampled diameter at every roof. Absence of reports is unknown, not no hail. A credible negative observation must identify who/what observed, where, when and its ability to detect hail.
7. **Reconcile, do not average.** A MESH estimate, a smaller nearby report and a later roof inspection answer different questions. Explain differences in timing, displacement, sampling, melting, spatial variability and method. Use "the hail-estimate grid/swath overlaps the job; surface hail remains unconfirmed" for a job intersecting an estimate, "hail reported near the job" for a nearby report, and inspection evidence for a damage conclusion. Do not derive a damage probability, impact energy or insurer eligibility from a swath.
8. **Make the next check specific.** Examples: a fresh scan to test persistence; another radar to address blockage; an exact product specification to decode a value; a time-matched surface report to address aloft-to-surface uncertainty; an onsite inspection to assess damage. Do not invent a generic numerical confidence score.

## Rain and QPE procedure

1. **Choose the right product for the decision.** For precipitation approaching a job, use a recent sequence and documented nowcast or conditional extrapolation. For water accumulated during an event, use QPE/gauges over aligned intervals. For scheduling tomorrow, use QPF/PoP with valid intervals. Mixing these is not an improvement in accuracy.
2. **Read the QPE lineage and flags.** Record radar-only, gauge-adjusted or multisensor method; contributing radar/gauges/models; precipitation phase; quality masks; accumulation interval; algorithm version; and revision availability. A gauge-adjusted estimate is not independently verified by a gauge already used in that adjustment. Multiple apps displaying that product do not create multiple observations.
3. **Challenge the rain signal.** Inspect bright-band/melting-layer contamination, mixed hail, beam blockage/overshoot, attenuation, clutter, missing radar input and mosaic seams. Warm-rain or tropical drop populations can differ from convective training examples. Dual-pol fields can help interpret rain versus hail, but KDP and ZDR are not universal rain-rate conversion tables. [S2, S3, S5] Small/no radar echoes can coexist with drizzle, shallow rain or precipitation below the sampled beam; never turn poor sampling into a dry-roof conclusion.
4. **Audit temporal arithmetic.** Prefer the supported accumulation product when available. Integrate rain-rate samples only with an explicit sampling/holding/interpolation method and actual time steps: amount in mm = sum(rate in mm/h × duration in hours). That result inherits the product's spatial and temporal uncertainties. Irregular sampling, gaps and rapidly changing cells matter. Never treat nodata as zero, extend the last rate indefinitely, sum overlapping rolling accumulations or silently bridge gaps. A known-interval subtotal must be labeled incomplete; do not call it the complete event amount. Do not average dBZ as though it were linear rainfall amount.
5. **Check independent gauges at the correct interval and scale.** Inspect clock/timezone, observation/reset period, units, QC, siting, blockage, wind-related catch error, intensity response and solid-precipitation effects as applicable to the instrument. A daily gauge ending at a morning observation time does not automatically match a midnight-to-midnight grid. Preserve trace amounts versus zero and explicit missing values. A point gauge can differ from a cell average because rain varies in space; report the representativeness issue before blaming one instrument.
6. **Use stage and drainage evidence for flooding.** Assess basin/upstream rainfall, prior wetness, terrain, urban impervious surfaces, burn scars, snowmelt, river stage, drainage and official warnings as relevant. Flooding may occur downstream away from rainfall, from ice/debris jams or infrastructure failure, and after rain has ended. [S4] Flash-flood guidance or a modeled hazard is guidance, not observed inundation. Do not infer that a road is passable from weak local radar or a low job-point rain total.
7. **Separate precipitation ending from a usable roof.** Rain can stop while substrate moisture, ponding, frost or condensation remain. Apply manufacturer/system requirements, work phase and onsite checks. This module does not estimate a universal drying time or issue a weather-based "safe to work" clearance.

## How to test improvement

Use the event-separated, decision-time-controlled evaluation protocol. Freeze matching rules and supported regions before examining scores. These are proposed checks; no real-event scores have been obtained for this module.

| Target | Reference evidence and matching | Report separately |
|---|---|---|
| Hail occurrence above a named size | Independently documented surface observations with credible detection ability; fixed time/location tolerances reflecting observation precision. Separate unresolved/unobserved areas. | Hits, misses, false alarms, valid negatives, abstentions and missing coverage; skill by radar range, terrain, storm type, season and region. No reports are not valid negatives. |
| Hail size | Matched size measurements whose quantity is comparable to the product target; record whether individual stone, local observed maximum or estimated object comparison. | Signed/absolute errors where comparison is defensible; size-class errors, censoring, unmatched cases and location/time uncertainty. A single observed stone does not establish the true storm maximum. |
| Rain amount/QPE | QC'd, interval-aligned independent gauges excluded from the product's adjustment when possible; predeclared spatial matching; identify gauge-versus-grid representativeness. | Bias, MAE/RMSE, threshold event detection and amount dependence; sample counts, excluded gauges, storm-cluster uncertainty, missing-data fraction and coverage. Do not divide by zero to compute relative error on dry cases. |
| Rain onset/end | Independent observations with known cadence and detection threshold; predeclared spatial/temporal match. | Early/late errors and missed wet periods; no precision finer than observing cadence can justify. |
| PoP/QPF | Forecasts actually available at the decision cutoff, with threshold, point/area, lead and interval fixed; independent outcomes. | Probability calibration/proper scores for actual numeric probabilities; forecast-amount errors separately. Do not score an updated forecast as the original one. |
| Flood outcome | Time-matched stream observations and validated inundation/impact evidence at appropriate sites/basins. | Flood detection/timing/extent separately from rainfall skill; retain non-rain causes and unobserved places. |

Compare the old and new reading procedures on identical inputs, blind evaluators to version when practical, and keep later reports/quality-controlled archives as outcomes rather than leaked earlier inputs. Do not describe multi-agent agreement, correct arithmetic or successful documentation retrieval as real-world hazard accuracy.

## Adversarial practice cases

These synthetic cases define expected reasoning, not observed storms or completed tests.

| ID | Challenge | Required response |
|---|---|---|
| HR01 | High reflectivity with rain-consistent co-located CC/ZDR/KDP; no size report. | Describe supported heavy-rain interpretation and remaining limits; do not name an exact hail diameter. |
| HR02 | Reduced CC in a melting-layer ring; no credible storm circulation. | Investigate wet/melting particles and quality; do not label hail or debris from CC alone. |
| HR03 | A freshly fetched 60-minute MESH maximum intersects a job; the storm passed 45 minutes earlier. | Report an estimate within that past interval; do not say hail is falling now. |
| HR04 | Two hail products use different algorithms/flavors; one has a percentile-style label. | Preserve each definition; do not average or turn the label into roof-level event probability. |
| HR05 | Radar estimate and a nearby smaller-hail report disagree; no parcel observation. | Preserve both scales/methods and unknown parcel conditions; do not fabricate a reconciled size or damage. |
| HR06 | 60 mm/h is observed for one five-minute interval only. | That interval contributes 5 mm under the stated constant-rate assumption; it does not prove 60 mm fell or will fall in an hour. |
| HR07 | Rolling 10:00–11:00 QPE is 12 mm; rolling 10:30–11:30 QPE is 18 mm. | Do not report 30 mm for 10:00–11:30; overlap prevents a total without additional information. |
| HR08 | Four five-minute rate intervals are available with one five-minute gap between them. | Report observed subtotal and missing coverage; do not impute dry weather or an unqualified complete total. |
| HR09 | A gauge is used in the multisensor QPE adjustment and closely matches that QPE. | Treat this as dependent agreement; seek withheld gauges for independent validation. |
| HR10 | A rain probability of 60% accompanies a 12-hour forecast. | Preserve probability/threshold/period; do not say 7.2 wet hours or 60% of roofs wet. |
| HR11 | Little rain at the job, but an upstream flash flood or ice-jam release is documented. | Preserve warning/impact evidence; local rain does not provide an all-clear. |
| HR12 | Radar is dry after rain, but no roof moisture observation exists. | State precipitation evidence and unresolved substrate condition; do not issue a dry-roof or installation clearance. |
| HR13 | A tropical-rain case has different polarimetric values from a classic hail case chart. | Use appropriate microphysical context and supported algorithm; do not force fixed chart thresholds. |
| HR14 | A 24-hour gauge ending at 07:00 is compared with a calendar-day total; a high-latitude job is outside a product mask. | Correct interval matching; preserve unavailable coverage rather than zero rainfall. |
| HR15 | A daily hail swath intersects a town struck by two storms; reports are revised the next day. | Separate event attribution and decision-time evidence; do not assign every report or swath maximum to either storm. |
