# Public-reporting checks

Status: proposed review procedure, not a broadcast certification. Public relay of an authoritative warning should not wait for AI analysis. Original consequential radar interpretation requires qualified meteorological and editorial review in a real publication workflow. No such reviewer or workflow has been established here.

## Evidence before wording

Record analysis as-of time, source observation/product interval, last successful retrieval, warning check time and local timezone with date/offset where ambiguous. Detect future timestamps and clock mismatch. A current mosaic header does not prove each tile is current. Keep stale/archive/simulation labels visible in graphics, headlines and clips, not only narration.

Evidence is authoritative for particular questions: official messages for warning status/instructions; radar for sampled volumes; quality-controlled surface measurements for their sites/times; verified reports for reported events; inspections for observed damage. There is no universal ranking that turns one category into another.

## Warning lifecycle

Track issuer, identifier, issuance/effective/expiry times, revision, geographic scope and official instructions. Resolve issuance, updates, corrections, cancellation, expiration and supersession using verified product-specific semantics. Distinguish watches, warnings and advisories. Handle polygon, point, county/zone and null-geometry cases without assuming a missing polygon means no affected area. Verify exact schemas before implementation.

Expiration is message status, not proof that a hazard ended. During an outage state: “The last verified warning was [message/status and validity]; current status could not be verified.” Do not extend an expired warning indefinitely or report “no warnings” from a failed request. A removed area on an updated polygon must not remain highlighted as currently warned without a clear historical label.

## Attribution ladder

These are different claims, not interchangeable synonyms:

- “Radar shows a feature consistent with rotation”: interpretation with limitations, not confirmed tornado.
- “The NWS warning describes a radar-indicated tornado”: an attributed official statement, matched to its current message/time/area.
- “A tornado was reported by [source] at [time/location]”: a report with verification/provenance status.
- “The NWS reports a confirmed tornado”: use only when that official message supports the wording and attribution.

Do not promote a radar estimate, social clip or repeated third-party report to official confirmation. A debris signature cannot establish casualties, tornado intensity or damage to a named property. Preserve hail-report precision and object-based estimates rather than adding decimal precision.

## Review gate

For an original public hazard interpretation, verify product identity, geographic applicability, age, field alignment, warning status and traceability of every claim. Confirm the image/headline implies no more certainty than the text. A qualified meteorologist reviews the scientific inference; a responsible editor reviews attribution, wording and correction readiness. Record identities, decisions and unresolved disagreements. If evidence or review is missing, withhold unsupported claims and narrow the statement to attributable verified information with its gap disclosed.

Radar or warning expiration cannot authorize “safe to resume roof work.” Lightning, residual wind, wet surfaces and site/company rules remain relevant and may be outside radar evidence. A green status card can imply clearance even when its caption is cautious; review visuals as claims.

Retain source snapshots where permitted, claim/evidence links, product/revision identifiers, timestamps, reviewer disposition and issued corrections. No source failure may silently become a reassuring statement.
