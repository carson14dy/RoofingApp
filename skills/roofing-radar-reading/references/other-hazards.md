# Weather beyond hail, rain and Doppler wind

Version 0.3.0 working guidance, reviewed 2026-10-11. These procedures broaden **evidence selection and error prevention**. They do not establish validated detection accuracy for every hazard. No live lightning, winter, heat, visibility, air-quality or tropical feed was connected or benchmarked for this revision.

Weather radar cannot supply an accurate reading of every weather phenomenon. Route each question to a suitable observing system and product. Retain official alerts on the independent warning path. A blank radar display, zero detections or expired warning never becomes a general statement that a roof or route is safe.

## Common evidence requirements

For every claim record what is being measured or inferred, where the source represents, its observation or valid time, uncertainty, quality flags and upstream lineage. Label these classes separately:

- **Observed:** an instrument measurement or a time/location-qualified human report; observations still have exposure, instrument and reporting errors.
- **Estimated:** a retrieval, analysis, classification or derived index. An index calculated from measured weather inputs remains a calculated index, not a measured roof temperature or human response.
- **Forecast:** a prediction with initialization, issuance, valid interval and lead time. A forecast field does not become observed because its valid time has arrived.
- **Official alert/guidance:** preserve issuer, hazard, geometry, issuance, validity, status and instructions. A warning is an official risk statement; it is not a local instrument measurement.
- **Onsite assessment:** qualified crew observations or specialist inspection, with time/location and scope. A weather exposure estimate cannot replace an assessment of structure, material, footing or occupational conditions.

Use product-specific age and latency limits documented before deployment, including an explicit maximum usable age. Source cadence, our polling interval and actual end-to-end age are different. Do not introduce one universal freshness cutoff for every hazard. Event-driven observations also require a feed heartbeat or another documented availability check: no received events is not proof of a healthy feed with no events.

## A map click is a question about a location

Resolve the requested coordinate, elevation where relevant, timezone, hazard and time mode before choosing evidence. A clicked point is not a sensor at that point. Label nearest-station distance/exposure and sampled grid cell/resolution; preserve actual alert geometry and location uncertainty. Interpolation or a grid cell containing the point does not turn an estimate into a measurement at the roof.

- **Current:** return the latest usable observations/estimates with their actual valid times and age, plus applicable official alerts. If only a nearby station is available, say what that station observed and what remains unknown at the job. A recent fetch cannot refresh an old observation.
- **Forecast:** return the forecast's issue/run time, requested valid interval, lead time, variable definition and grid/area support. The interval may be underway; historical forecast replay must preserve availability at the chosen cutoff. A forecast remains a forecast after its valid time passes. Keep warnings distinct. Do not label a forecast value “reading” or “measured” at the clicked point.
- **Past:** use archived observations/estimates and preserve accumulation windows, record revisions and the historical warning state. Distinguish evidence available in real time from later analyses. A nearby historic lightning/ice/flood report does not prove a property impact; an absent report does not establish none occurred.

When the selected product lacks coverage or suitable resolution for the requested hazard/time, return unavailable/insufficient with the reason and an appropriate source alternative. Never fill an unsupported map cell with zero hazard or silently substitute simulated values.

## How, what, where, when and why

The table is a routing procedure, not a promise of geographic coverage or a set of crew clearance rules. Verify each chosen product's actual domain for CONUS, Alaska, Hawaii and territories separately, including mountain, coastal and offshore gaps. National agency branding does not establish nationwide product coverage.

| Hazard / what claim is supportable | How: required evidence and method | Where: representation and coverage | When: time support and review triggers | Why: roofing use and independent truth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lightning / thunderstorm electrical activity** | Use a documented lightning network with detection type, location uncertainty, quality and latency. Distinguish optical satellite detections, intracloud activity and classified cloud-to-ground records. Pair with current official guidance and thunder heard onsite. Reflectivity is context, not a lightning sensor. | Check network coverage, detection efficiency and sensor geometry. A satellite optical flash location/extent is not a confirmed ground contact or a precise strike at a building. Do not turn a grid or detection-error area into a property-impact footprint. | Use event time and receipt time; flag delayed bursts, feed outages and stale displays. Review on new nearby activity, thunder, storm development or loss of coverage. NWS outdoor guidance says to wait 30 minutes after the last rumble of thunder before resuming activity; this specific lightning guidance is not a general roof-work clearance. | Plan shelter access and lead time. Do not wait for a severe warning: lightning can occur without one. Independent verification needs a genuinely separate observing network or a suitable ground validation system; thunder is evidence of nearby lightning, not an exact location or strike count. |
| **Snow, sleet, freezing rain and freezing drizzle** | Combine radar/dual-pol sampling height with the vertical temperature and moisture profile, surface temperature/wet-bulb context, present-weather observations and official winter guidance. Label precipitation-type algorithms as estimates and model profiles as forecasts/analyses. Actual icing needs surface evidence. | Strongly dependent on terrain, shallow cold layers, radar range and site elevation. A warm layer aloft can coexist with freezing conditions below; nearby airport observations may miss a valley or exposed roof. Document each model and winter product's domain. | Track onset, transitions, profile evolution and accumulation intervals. A prior snow report does not fix the current precipitation type. Very shallow drizzle or light snow may be poorly sampled even with a nominally healthy radar. Review when temperature/profile or onsite conditions change. | Anticipate icing, loss of traction, access and dry-in problems. Verify type with time-matched trained reports/present-weather sensors, and accumulation with appropriate snow/ice measurements. Instrument limitations and report precision remain part of truth quality. |
| **Snow/ice loading, frost and roof wetness** | Use snow depth, snow-water equivalent (SWE), ice observations and site/structure information for the appropriate assessment. Frost/condensation assessment needs roof/substrate conditions and moisture context; a forecast air temperature is insufficient. Never substitute wind chill for surface temperature. | Snow redistribution, drifting, roof geometry, heat loss, drainage and elevation make a regional grid unsuitable for certifying roof load or footing. Ice accretion measurement conventions differ by surface/instrument. | State the accumulation interval and distinguish new snowfall from existing snowpack/SWE. Loading and wetness can persist after precipitation ends; rain-on-snow and melt/refreeze require continued assessment. | Support inspection and qualified structural/site assessment. A fixed snowfall-to-load or snow-to-liquid conversion is not a measurement. Independent truth is appropriately measured SWE/load/ice or a competent onsite assessment; radar alone cannot establish capacity, dry substrate or a slip-free roof. |
| **Heat and solar exposure** | Use quality-controlled air temperature and humidity, sun/radiation, wind and a documented heat-stress method. Keep measured WBGT distinct from modeled/estimated WBGT, and both distinct from heat index. Preserve applicable method assumptions; consult the employer's heat program and workload/PPE/acclimatization criteria. | A station over representative ground can differ from a sunlit roof. Heat index represents shady/light-wind assumptions; it is not a measured rooftop heat burden. Verify any forecast product's geographic domain, operating status and limitations. | Align input measurements and forecast intervals; use event-time maxima correctly. Review changing sun, wind, workload and site measurements. An overnight forecast minimum does not describe the next afternoon's exposure. | Plan workload and shelter/rest arrangements under the relevant program. Validate with calibrated onsite instruments of the same quantity; avoid comparing forecast WBGT directly against measured air temperature. No universal heat-index or WBGT work/stop threshold is supplied here. |
| **Cold exposure** | Keep actual air temperature, wind chill and surface/substrate temperature separate. NWS wind chill is an exposed-skin cooling index, not an object-temperature calculation; use the documented formula only in its supported domain. Cold, wetness, clothing and exposure duration need relevant work guidance. | Wind exposure differs at roof height and sheltered stations; local terrain and site conditions matter. Cold alert criteria vary locally. | Respect synchronized input times and gust-versus-sustained definitions. Review after wetting, wind changes and material-temperature changes. | Inform exposure planning and material checks separately. Validate the weather inputs against representative observations and substrate claims against substrate measurements. Wind chill alone cannot show that materials or water are below freezing. |
| **Fog, low cloud and visibility** | Use visibility observations, present weather, official fog guidance and appropriate satellite/model context. Distinguish horizontal visibility, cloud ceiling and a satellite low-cloud/fog classification. Radar reflectivity is an inadequate primary visibility measurement. | Airport visibility represents its observing site and method; conditions can vary sharply along routes, over water and through valleys. Satellite low cloud does not uniquely establish fog at the surface beneath it. | Use valid observation time and changing ceilings/visibility; fog can form or dissipate locally between reports. Review observations along the route and at the job, not just the nearest station. | Plan access and lifting/working visibility under site rules. Independent truth: calibrated visibility observations or quality-controlled local observations. A webcam must have known time, view, lighting and adequate contrast before supporting even a qualitative visibility statement. |
| **Flooding, ponding and coastal inundation** | Pair rainfall estimates/forecasts with official flood alerts, stream/tide/water-level observations, relevant hydrologic guidance, drainage/topography and site reports. Distinguish observed gauge stage, modeled discharge, forecast inundation and reported road flooding. Rain rate alone is not flood depth. | Gauge observations apply to their site/reach and datum. Downstream, upstream, urban drainage and coastal sites respond differently. River/flash/coastal flood products have different domains and geometries; do not infer one from another. | Floodwater can rise or remain hazardous after local rain ends. Match accumulation intervals, river forecast issue/valid times and tidal timing; review water-level changes and alert updates. | Plan access and protect open work areas under official directions. Validate with independent gauges or documented inundation observations. A radar pixel cannot show a passable road, roof drain capacity or water depth at a property. |
| **Tropical cyclones and their multiple hazards** | Preserve current NHC/CPHC and local NWS products appropriate to the basin, including wind, rainfall/flood, surge and tornado guidance. Use radar and observations for sampled local evolution. Treat track, wind-probability, arrival-time and inundation products according to their own definitions. | Resolve the responsible center and local office per basin/territory; NHC/CPHC do not cover every U.S. tropical-cyclone jurisdiction. Verify western North Pacific products for Guam/CNMI instead of routing every tropical request through NHC/CPHC. A track cone concerns center-track uncertainty and is not the storm's full impact area. Hazards extend beyond the cone and far inland. Basin responsibility, local terrain and product domain must be explicit; never extrapolate a CONUS radar product across islands or open ocean without coverage. | Keep forecast cycle, valid horizon and revisions. Do not confuse arrival of tropical-storm-force winds with the center's landfall time. Impacts can precede landfall and persist after weakening. | Coordinate preparation/access decisions with official local instructions. Verify surface wind with suitable stations, rainfall with gauges and inundation with water-level/field evidence; best-track analyses are retrospective estimates with revisions, not property truth. |
| **Dust, smoke, air quality and fire-weather context** | Use official dust/fire-weather alerts, visibility and surface wind for dust; measured pollutant concentrations and a documented AQI method for air quality; satellite/fire detections and dispersion forecasts only in their correct evidence classes. An elevated plume is not a surface concentration measurement. | Check pollutant, monitor placement, vertical plume position, terrain and transport. AQI coverage and averaging periods differ from a point instantaneous reading. A radar fine line or satellite hot spot does not establish an active fire at a roof. | Review rapid outflow dust, changing plume transport, pollutant averaging/NowCast intervals and feed age. Red Flag guidance describes conditions favorable for fire behavior; it does not confirm an ignition. | Support travel and occupational exposure review. Independent truth: calibrated surface pollutant and visibility measurements plus verified fire reports. Fire perimeter, evacuation and health/occupational decisions require the appropriate authority. No generic AQI-to-roof-work clearance is supplied. |

## Proxy failures that must stop an unsupported claim

1. **No echo means no lightning, drizzle, fog, cold or heat:** reject. Radar availability and a no-echo result do not observe these hazards adequately.
2. **A detected lightning flash equals a ground strike:** reject unless the product actually identifies the relevant ground event and its uncertainty supports the claim. Different providers may copy one network; duplicate feeds are not independent truth.
3. **Rain-colored radar plus 34°F air temperature means no icing:** reject. Near-surface profile, surface/substrate temperature, wet-bulb effects and local conditions may differ; color often comes from an algorithm/model and is not observed phase at the roof.
4. **A wind chill of 25°F means a roof is at 25°F:** reject. Wind chill does not measure an object. Also avoid the reverse claim that a roof cannot be colder than ambient air: radiation and evaporation can change surface temperature independently of the wind-chill index.
5. **A winter accumulation maximum is current precipitation intensity:** reject. Preserve period, variable and units; snow depth, SWE, snowfall and glaze thickness are different quantities.
6. **The nearest airport is clear, so every route/job is clear:** reject. Surface hazards can be highly local; source representativeness is part of the uncertainty.
7. **A clear radar screen or stopped rain means flooding is over:** reject. Catchment response, upstream rain, dam/drainage behavior and tide can decouple flood timing from local rainfall.
8. **A site outside the hurricane cone has no hazard:** reject. Preserve each hazard's own geometry and official guidance.
9. **Satellite smoke establishes unhealthy air at the roof:** reject without surface concentration evidence or properly labeled, validated guidance. Conversely, no visible smoke does not establish clean air.
10. **A public educational calculator is an operational feed:** reject. Verify product status, terms, supported methods and service contract. The NWS Tulsa WBGT page retrieved in this review explicitly labels its calculator a prototype not for operational use.

## Adversarial review cases and required outcomes

These are synthetic challenges, not observed storms and not an accuracy benchmark. A reviewer must ask what supports the claim, how it was derived, where it applies, when it was valid and why the proposed action follows.

| Case | Tempting wrong answer | Required outcome / next evidence |
|---|---|---|
| OH-01: Lightning API returns no events after its heartbeat stops; radar is clear | No lightning risk | Mark lightning feed unavailable; preserve any valid official information and onsite thunder evidence. Do not start an automated all-clear clock from a disconnected stream. |
| OH-02: Satellite optical lightning flash spans a job but no classified ground record exists | Roof struck | State optical lightning activity over/near the area within the product's precision; ground contact and property strike are unconfirmed. |
| OH-03: Crew hears thunder while the licensed detector shows no nearby events | Detector proves it is safe to continue | Apply current official lightning guidance and the site's plan; investigate latency, coverage and classification. An absence of detections cannot negate thunder. |
| OH-04: Reflectivity is absent at long range but an airport reports freezing drizzle | No precipitation | Preserve the report with location/time; identify shallow-precipitation/coverage limitations and seek representative jobsite conditions. |
| OH-05: Radar samples snow above a warm layer, with a shallow freezing layer below | Snow on the roof | Surface type remains uncertain until vertical-profile and surface evidence support a diagnosis; distinguish sleet/freezing-rain possibilities. |
| OH-06: 6 inches of snowfall reported, contractor asks for roof load | Use a fixed snow density | Do not calculate a certified load from snowfall alone; obtain relevant SWE/density, drift/site/structure evidence and qualified assessment. |
| OH-07: Air 36°F, wind chill 27°F | Roof ice confirmed by wind chill | Wind chill is not substrate temperature. Seek actual roof/surface and moisture observations; do not rule out radiative frost solely from ambient air either. |
| OH-08: Forecast heat index is moderate, crew is doing strenuous work on a sunlit roof in PPE | Comfortable/safe to work | Explain the index's assumptions and need for suitable heat-stress/site/work criteria. Do not fabricate WBGT or a work/rest schedule. |
| OH-09: A modeled WBGT grid is labeled “WBGT observation” in the display | Measured heat stress | Correct the evidence class to estimate/forecast; retain method and valid time. Measured WBGT and modeled WBGT require separate validation. |
| OH-10: Airport visibility is good; job is in a foggy valley | All routes and roof visibility good | Limit the airport claim to its site/time; obtain route/job evidence and current official guidance. |
| OH-11: Rain ended locally but upstream gauges are rising | Flood threat over | Retain flood information and downstream uncertainty; retrieve relevant hydrologic/official updates. |
| OH-12: Job outside tropical track cone but inside official wind guidance | Outside storm threat | Interpret the applicable hazard product and its time window; cone location does not cancel wind, flood or tornado concern. |
| OH-13: A smoke plume is overhead; a surface monitor is far upwind | Current roof AQI known | State plume observation separately; surface exposure at the job is unresolved. Do not transplant the upwind reading or invent AQI. |
| OH-14: Dust boundary appears on radar without surface observations | Exact visibility and gust inferred | Describe candidate outflow/dust context with quality limits; request official warning and surface/visibility evidence. |
| OH-15: A product offers CONUS coverage while a job is in Hawaii | Zero hazard because the grid is empty | Mark product unavailable for that location; use appropriate local/basin evidence and preserve the gap. |
| OH-16: Two apps agree on ice or lightning but both use one upstream dataset | Independent confirmation | Count one lineage, retain both transformations if useful, and seek an independent observation. |

## Required verification before claiming accuracy

Evaluate each hazard and each supported region/season separately. A CONUS hail benchmark does not validate Alaska freezing rain, Hawaiian rainfall or a tropical island's wind/surge exposure. Include ordinary weather, low-coverage locations, rapid transitions, mixed precipitation, sensor outages and out-of-domain requests.

Use truth appropriate to the claim: representative ground lightning observations for ground-strike claims, present-weather observations for precipitation type, calibrated site indices for heat stress estimates, surface visibility for visibility, and gauges/field observations for inundation. Record independent-observation availability; a missing report is not a verified negative. Preserve measurement error, representativeness and time/location matching error instead of calling all references perfect ground truth.

Report missed events, false alarms, timing/location error and reliability/calibration where the forecast form supports them, stratified by coverage and domain. Validate numerical physical quantities against like quantities with defined units and intervals. Use held-out events and shadow operation before operational claims, and retain human meteorological review for public reporting. No scores or passed real-event tests are claimed by this document.
